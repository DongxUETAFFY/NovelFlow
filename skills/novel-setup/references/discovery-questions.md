# Structured Discovery Questions

Ask these questions as a **single batched message** (don't interrogate one at a time). Adapt wording to what the user already told you. Skip any question the user has already answered.

## Core Questions

1. **Genre & Tone**: What genre(s)? What's the emotional tone — dark and gritty, hopeful and warm, tense thriller, humorous, melancholic, literary?

2. **POV & Narrative Voice**: First person or third? Single or multiple POV? Past or present tense? Reliable or unreliable narrator?

3. **Protagonist**: Who is the main character? What do they **want** (external goal)? What do they actually **need** (internal need)? What's their flaw or blind spot?

4. **Antagonist / Opposition**: Who or what opposes the protagonist? A villain, a system, nature, their own self?

5. **Plot Shape**: Do you have a sense of the three-act structure? The inciting incident? A midpoint twist? The climax? If not, we can discover these together.

6. **World-Building**: Real world (contemporary or historical), or invented setting? Does it need magic systems, future tech, or special rules?

7. **Scope & Audience**: Roughly how many chapters? Target audience? Any content boundaries to respect?

## Engagement

For each answer, engage with genuine curiosity — help the user discover their story, don't prescribe. If they're unsure about plot structure, offer gentle prompts: "Let's think about the inciting incident — what's the moment that pushes your protagonist out of their normal world and into the story?"

If something is vague ("I want it to be mysterious"), ask: "Mysterious in what way — a whodunit with hidden clues? Cosmic horror where reality is uncertain? A character with a concealed past?"
